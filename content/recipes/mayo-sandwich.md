## The Mayo Sandwich That Reminds Me to Slow Down

Some recipes come from generations of carefully guarded family traditions. Others are passed down in stained recipe cards tucked into antique cookbooks, their corners softened by decades of loving use.

And then there's this mayo sandwich.

I still remember the first time I had one. It was a humid Tuesday in late August, though I couldn't tell you the year anymore. Summer was winding down, the cicadas were singing outside my grandmother's kitchen window, and the scent of freshly cut grass drifted through the screen door. I had spent the entire morning doing absolutely nothing productive—a skill children seem to possess effortlessly and adults spend years trying to relearn.

My grandmother looked into the pantry, then into the refrigerator, then back into the pantry again with the sort of quiet confidence that only comes from decades of feeding people. There wasn't much to work with. A loaf of soft white bread. A jar of mayonnaise. That was enough.

"Sometimes," she said, "the simplest meals are the ones you remember."

At the time, I thought she was just trying to make lunch sound exciting.

She spread the mayonnaise with remarkable care, all the way to the edges of the bread. No dry corners. Every bite mattered. She gently pressed the slices together, cut the sandwich diagonally—as all truly comforting sandwiches should be—and slid it across the table.

I took one bite.

Creamy. Tangy. Pillowy soft. It wasn't trying to impress anyone. It wasn't topped with artisanal microgreens or drizzled with infused olive oil or stacked seventeen ingredients high for social media. It was simply bread and mayonnaise, existing together in perfect harmony.

Years passed.

Life became busier. Jobs changed. Apartments came and went. Kitchens grew larger and then smaller again. I learned to make elaborate dinners that dirtied every pot I owned. I experimented with homemade pasta, slow-fermented sourdough, and sauces that simmered for six hours.

But somehow, on the longest days, I always found myself returning to the mayo sandwich.

It became my rainy-day lunch.

My "the grocery store trip can wait until tomorrow" meal.

My "I've spent three hours cooking for other people and now I just want something for myself" comfort food.

Friends laugh when I tell them about it.

"That's the recipe?" they ask.

"That's the recipe," I reply.

Because not every recipe needs twenty ingredients. Not every meal has to become a project. Sometimes nourishment is as much about familiarity as it is about flavor.

I know this recipe won't win awards. It won't be featured on restaurant menus. You probably won't see celebrity chefs competing over who can make the world's best mayo sandwich.

But maybe that's exactly why I love it.

It's honest.

It's dependable.

It's there for you when the refrigerator is nearly empty and payday is still two days away. It's there when you're sick. It's there when you're nostalgic. It's there when you just need lunch and don't feel like pretending otherwise.

Food doesn't always have to astonish us.

Sometimes it just has to show up.

So today I'm sharing one of the most requested "recipes" among my closest friends—not because they couldn't figure it out, but because every time they came over and saw me making one, they'd ask, "Why does yours somehow look better?"

The secret, I've decided, isn't technique.

It's confidence.

Spread the mayonnaise generously. Don't apologize for it. Cover every inch of the bread. Cut it however your heart desires. Eat it immediately while the bread is still impossibly soft.

And if someone asks why you're eating a mayonnaise sandwich, simply smile and tell them it's a family recipe.

Technically, after enough years, it is.

## Ingredients

* 2 slices soft white bread
* 2–3 tablespoons mayonnaise

<br/>

## Instructions

1. Lay the bread slices on a clean surface.
2. Spread the mayonnaise evenly over one or both slices, making sure to reach all the edges.
3. Press the slices together.
4. Slice in half if desired.
5. Serve immediately and enjoy.
